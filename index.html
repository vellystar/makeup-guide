✦ Know Your Skin

Combination Skin Guide

Understanding your skin type is the foundation of every good routine.

✦ You Have Combination Skin

Combination skin is characterized by an oily T-zone (forehead, nose, and chin) alongside normal-to-dry cheeks and outer areas. This is one of the most common skin types, requiring a balanced approach — controlling shine where needed while maintaining hydration elsewhere.

🔬
Characteristics
  • Oily T-zone (forehead, nose, chin)
  • Normal to dry cheeks & temples
  • Enlarged pores around the nose
  • Prone to blackheads in T-zone
  • Makeup tends to slide off T-zone
  • Dry patches may appear on cheeks
  • Skin changes with seasons & humidity
🧪
Key Ingredients to Look For
  • Niacinamide — Balances oil, minimizes pores
  • Hyaluronic Acid — Hydrates without heaviness
  • Ceramides — Strengthens skin barrier
  • Salicylic Acid — Unclogs pores (spot-use)
  • Green Tea Extract — Controls sebum
  • Zinc — Regulates oil production
  • Vitamin C — Brightens & evens tone
🚫
Ingredients to Avoid
  • Heavy oils (coconut, mineral oil) on T-zone
  • Alcohol-heavy toners (strips skin)
  • Thick, occlusive creams on oily areas
  • Harsh scrubs (damages barrier)
  • Silicone-heavy formulas (can clog pores)
  • Overly mattifying products on dry zones
🌤️
Morning Skin Habits
  • Gentle cleanser — don't over-strip
  • Lightweight hydrating toner
  • Niacinamide serum on T-zone
  • Light moisturizer (gel or lotion formula)
  • SPF 30–50 daily — non-negotiable
  • Blot T-zone before makeup
  • Primer on oily zones only
🌙
Evening Skin Habits
  • Double cleanse — oil cleanser first
  • Hydrating toner/essence
  • Treatment serums (Vitamin C, retinol)
  • Eye cream if needed
  • Richer moisturizer on dry zones
  • Lightweight gel on oily zones
  • Never sleep with makeup on
💄
Makeup Tips for Combo Skin
  • Use a mattifying or pore-minimizing primer
  • Apply foundation in thin, buildable layers
  • Powder the T-zone only
  • Avoid over-powdering dry cheeks
  • Setting spray locks everything in
  • Blotting papers > reapplying powder mid-day
  • Cream products can be patchy on oily skin

Undertone Reference Guide

🌅
Warm Undertone
Yellow, peachy, or golden hues beneath skin. Looks best in gold jewelry. Veins appear green. Best shades: beige, caramel, honey, warm ivory, vanilla.
🌊
Cool Undertone
Pink, red, or bluish hues beneath skin. Looks best in silver jewelry. Veins appear blue/purple. Best shades: porcelain, rose beige, pink-toned ivory.
⚖️
Neutral Undertone
Mix of warm and cool. Flattering in both gold & silver. Veins appear blue-green. Most flexible shade range — can wear both warm and cool tones.
💌
Your Profile
Based on your shades (Vanilla, 17C Porcelain), you lean neutral-to-cool, fair to light depth. The 17C indicates a cool preference for base, while Vanilla adds warm flexibility.

✅ Do

  • Multi-mask (clay on T-zone, hydrating mask on cheeks)
  • Use separate products for different zones
  • Blot excess oil throughout the day
  • Stay hydrated — dehydration worsens oiliness
  • Use SPF every single day
  • Cleanse gently — twice a day maximum
  • Choose non-comedogenic makeup formulas

⚠️ Avoid

  • Over-washing — triggers more oil production
  • Skipping moisturizer on oily areas
  • Heavy, creamy foundations on T-zone
  • Applying heavy powder all over face
  • Touching your face throughout the day
  • Using the same product on all zones
  • Skipping SPF to avoid breakouts

✦ Foundation

Types of Foundation

Foundation is the base of every makeup look. Understanding each formula helps you choose the right one for your skin, occasion, and desired finish.

💧
Liquid Foundation
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

The most versatile formula. Water or silicone base with pigment suspended throughout. Available in every coverage level and finish — sheer, medium, or full; matte, satin, or dewy.

  • Best for: All skin types · Customisable coverage
  • Finish: Varies by formula
  • How to apply: Pump onto fingertips or a damp beauty blender. Dot onto forehead, cheeks, nose, chin, and blend outward from the centre. Use circular buffing for full coverage; stippling for lighter, skin-like coverage. Work quickly before it starts to set.
  • Combo skin tip: Use a mattifying liquid on T-zone and a hydrating formula on cheeks, or blend both together for balance.
🪞
Cushion Foundation
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

A liquid formula saturated into a sponge cushion and applied with a puff. Delivers a skin-like, buildable coverage with a natural-to-glowing finish. Extremely popular in Korean beauty.

  • Best for: Normal · Dry · Combination skin
  • Finish: Natural, satin, or glowing
  • How to apply: Press puff gently into the cushion (don't rub). Pat — never swipe — from the centre of the face outward. Build in thin layers. Clean the puff regularly to prevent product buildup. Apply lighter on T-zone, build more on cheeks and areas needing coverage.
  • Your products: TIRTIR Mask Fit · SKINTIFIC Cover Glow · TIMEPHORIA Lumina Matte
🌫️
Powder Foundation
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ✗Combo ~Normal ~Sensitive ~

Pigment suspended in powder form — pressed or loose. Provides light-to-medium coverage with an instant matte finish. Great for quick touch-ups and oily skin control but can look cakey on dry skin.

  • Best for: Oily · Combination (T-zone) · Quick application
  • Finish: Matte to soft-matte
  • How to apply: Use a flat powder puff for fuller coverage or a large powder brush for sheer. Press into skin and buff in circular motions. Always apply to prepped, moisturised skin. Avoid over-applying on dry areas.
  • Combo skin tip: Use only on T-zone if used as a finishing product over liquid base.
🧴
Serum Foundation / Skin Tint
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

A hybrid of skincare and makeup — infused with active ingredients like hyaluronic acid, niacinamide, or vitamins. Delivers sheer to light coverage with a skin-blurring effect that improves skin over time with consistent use.

  • Best for: Normal · Dry · Sensitive · Good skin days
  • Finish: Natural, luminous, skin-like
  • How to apply: Pump a small amount and blend with fingertips (warmth from fingers helps merge with skin) or a damp beauty blender. Press into skin rather than buffing. Layer with a cushion over it for added coverage where needed.
  • Your product: ESQA Minimalist Blurring Serum Skin Tint
🧈
BB Cream / CC Cream
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

BB (Blemish Balm): Tinted moisturiser with light coverage and skin benefits. CC (Colour Correcting): Corrects redness and uneven tone while providing coverage. Both sit lighter than foundation and work as multi-tasking daily base products.

  • Best for: All skin types · Minimal makeup days
  • Finish: Natural, skin-like
  • How to apply: Apply with fingers using press-and-blend motions. Can be used all over or just on uneven areas. Blend edges at the hairline and jaw carefully. Layer concealer on top for targeted coverage.
  • Your product: ONLYOU Concealer Brightening & Isolating BB Cream
✨
Stick Foundation
Skin TypeOily ✗Dry ✓Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ~

Solid foundation in a stick or bullet form. High coverage, transfer-resistant, and very portable. Can be creamy or waxy in texture. Great for spot-application and on-the-go use. Sets to a semi-matte or satin finish.

  • Best for: Normal · Dry · Full coverage needs
  • Finish: Satin to semi-matte
  • How to apply: Swipe directly onto the face in short strokes. Blend immediately with a beauty blender or brush — it sets quickly. Use lightly on T-zone for combo skin as the creamy formula can emphasise oiliness.
  • Combo skin tip: Set with translucent powder immediately after blending to prevent sliding.
📊
Coverage Levels Explained
  • Sheer: Skin shows through — evens tone without hiding features. Best for great skin days. (ESQA Skin Tint)
  • Light: Slightly more pigment — softens imperfections, not hides them. (ONLYOU BB Cream)
  • Medium: Covers most blemishes and redness with one to two layers. Most versatile everyday coverage. (SKINTIFIC · TIMEPHORIA)
  • Full: Completely masks blemishes, discolouration, and texture. One layer does the work. (TIRTIR Mask Fit)
  • Buildable: Starts light and builds to full coverage with layers — the most flexible approach. (Most cushions)

✦ Concealer

Types of Concealer

Concealer provides targeted, spot coverage where foundation alone isn't enough — under-eyes, blemishes, redness, and hyperpigmentation.

💧
Liquid Concealer
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

The most common formula — fluid consistency that blends seamlessly and sets to a natural or matte finish. Available in full or medium coverage. The most versatile type for all areas of the face.

  • Best for: Under-eyes · Blemishes · All skin types
  • How to apply: Dot under the eye in an inverted triangle shape, reaching down to the top of the cheekbone. Pat — never drag — with the ring finger or a damp beauty blender. For blemishes, apply a small dot and stipple the edges to blend. Set with a light dusting of translucent powder to prevent creasing.
  • Combo skin tip: Avoid over-applying around the nose — can settle into pores.
🖊️
Stick / Pencil Concealer
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ✓Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ~

Solid concealer in stick or pencil form. Very high coverage, easy to target specific spots. More creamy and less blendable than liquid — better for isolated blemishes than large areas like under-eyes.

  • Best for: Spot coverage · Blemishes · Dark spots · Travel
  • How to apply: Apply directly to the blemish or spot. Tap with a fingertip to blend the edges into the surrounding skin. Don't rub — this removes the product from where you need it most. Set with powder to extend wear.
  • Your product: ONLYOU Concealer & Isolating BB Cream (dual-function)
🟡
Colour Correcting Concealer
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

Uses colour theory to neutralise specific discolourations before applying skin-toned concealer on top. Each corrector colour cancels a specific tone: peach/orange cancels purple/blue dark circles; green cancels red; yellow cancels purple veins.

  • Peach/Salmon: Under-eye dark circles (fair to medium skin)
  • Orange: Under-eye circles (medium to deep skin)
  • Green: Redness, blemishes, broken capillaries
  • Yellow: Purple veins, bruising, dull skin
  • How to apply: Apply a thin layer of corrector first, press into skin, let it settle, then apply your regular concealer on top. Always use less than you think you need — correctors are highly pigmented.
🧴
Brightening Concealer
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

Contains light-reflecting particles or illuminating agents to brighten and lift the under-eye area. Not just for coverage — creates a luminous, awake effect. Often a shade or two lighter than your foundation shade.

  • Best for: Under-eyes · Brow bone highlight · Bridge of nose · Inner corners
  • How to apply: Apply in an inverted triangle from the inner corner of the eye down to the top of the cheekbone. Blend upward. Also tap onto the brow bone and inner corner of the eye. For a natural look, use only one shade lighter than your skin. For a highlighted look, go two shades lighter and set with a shimmer powder.
  • Your product: ONLYOU Concealer Brightening & Isolating BB Cream (brightening function)
🔺
Concealer Application Order & Techniques
  • After foundation: Always apply concealer after foundation — foundation provides a base that reduces how much concealer you need, preventing a cakey result.
  • The inverted triangle: For under-eyes, apply in an upside-down triangle shape (not just a crescent under the eye). This brightens the full under-eye area and creates a natural lifted look.
  • Baking technique: Apply concealer, press a generous amount of translucent powder on top, leave for 2–3 minutes, then dust off. Sets concealer for all-day crease-free wear — ideal for combo skin.
  • Blending tools: Ring finger = softest pressure for under-eyes. Small brush = precision on blemishes. Damp beauty blender = most natural, seamless finish overall.
  • Setting: Always set concealer with Second Date Translucent Powder to prevent creasing, especially under the eyes.

✦ Highlighter

Types of Highlighter

Highlighter catches and reflects light to create the illusion of lifted, glowing, three-dimensional skin. Placement is everything.

✨
Powder Highlighter
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ~Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

The most common type — pressed shimmer powder applied with a brush. Intense, buildable, and easy to control. Creates a bold, noticeable glow. Available in fine shimmer (natural) or chunky glitter (editorial).

  • Best for: Oily · Combination · Layering over powder makeup
  • Placement: Tops of cheekbones · Brow bone · Bridge of nose · Cupid's bow · Inner corners of eyes · Tip of nose (optional)
  • How to apply: Use a small fan brush or tapered highlighter brush. Lightly swirl into the pan, tap off excess, and apply with a light feathery stroke on the highest points of the face. Build gradually — you can always add more but you can't take it back. For intense highlight, use a flat finger or brush to press and pack the product.
  • Combo skin tip: Apply only on dry cheekbone area — avoid the T-zone entirely to prevent looking oily rather than glowy.
💧
Liquid / Oil Highlighter
Skin TypeOily ✗Dry ✓Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

Gives a natural, skin-from-within glow that looks less like product and more like genuinely luminous skin. Can be mixed into foundation, applied directly to skin, or pressed onto cheekbones. The most natural-looking highlight formula.

  • Best for: Dry · Normal · "No makeup makeup" looks
  • Placement: Cheekbones · Temples · Bridge of nose · Eyelids · Collarbones
  • How to apply: Warm 1–2 drops between fingertips and press gently onto the tops of cheekbones. Alternatively, mix one drop into your foundation before application for an all-over luminous base. For targeted application, use a flat brush to press onto specific areas after foundation is set.
  • Your product: NPure Power Series Glow Up Oil — Marigold Peach on cheekbones creates a warm, sun-kissed oil-highlighter effect. Apply only on cheeks and dry zones.
🧴
Cream Highlighter
Skin TypeOily ✗Dry ✓Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

A creamy, balm-like formula that delivers an intense, molten-skin glow. Blends into skin before setting, leaving a luminous satin finish. More intense than liquid but softer than powder. Best applied before powder products.

  • Best for: Dry · Normal · Dewy makeup looks
  • Placement: Tops of cheekbones · Inner eye corners · Brow bone · Cupid's bow
  • How to apply: Dab with a fingertip directly onto cheekbones after foundation but before powder. Blend with a tapping motion — not rubbing — to work it into the skin. It should look seamless, not patchy. Set lightly with a fine translucent powder if you have oily skin.
  • Combo skin tip: Apply before setting powder — cream under powder creates a natural-looking glow that doesn't slide. Avoid on T-zone.
🌟
Highlighter Shade Guide

Choosing the right highlight shade for your undertone creates a natural, skin-harmonious glow rather than looking mismatched or ashy.

  • Warm undertone: Gold, bronze, peach, copper — warm metallic shades harmonise naturally. (Suits you with Vanilla and Marigold Peach)
  • Cool undertone: Silver, icy pink, rose gold, lavender — cool light tones. (Suits your 17C Porcelain shade)
  • Neutral undertone: Champagne, soft gold, nude shimmer — the most versatile. Flattering on nearly everyone.
  • JADED Palette highlight shades: Shade 01 Butter Cream (champagne shimmer) works as an eye highlight. Shade 05 Copper Glow can double as a warm cheek highlight.

✦ Contour

Types of Contour

Contouring uses shadow to create the illusion of depth, definition, and dimension — sculpting the face without changing its actual structure.

🖌️
Powder Contour
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ~Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

The most beginner-friendly type — a cool-toned matte powder applied with a brush. Buildable, blendable, and easy to correct mistakes. Sits over powder makeup seamlessly. Matte is essential — any shimmer in a contour reads as highlight rather than shadow.

  • Best for: Oily · Combination · Beginners · Everyday use
  • Shade rule: Always 2–3 shades darker than your foundation with a cool, grey-brown (not orange or warm) undertone to mimic natural shadow
  • Placement: Below cheekbones · Temples · Jawline · Sides of nose · Hairline
  • How to apply: Use a angled contour brush or fluffy blending brush. Suck in cheeks and apply the product in the hollow below the cheekbone. Blend upward toward the temples. For the jaw, apply underneath the jawline and blend downward onto the neck. For the nose, apply two thin lines down the sides using a small brush and blend with a fingertip. Always blend — no harsh lines.
  • Combo skin tip: Powder contour is ideal — it naturally absorbs oil and layers well over matte base products like TIMEPHORIA and Second Date Powder.
🧴
Cream / Stick Contour
Skin TypeOily ✗Dry ✓Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ~

Intense, highly pigmented formula that blends into skin before setting. Creates a more natural, skin-melded shadow effect than powder. More difficult to blend but more photo-realistic. Applied before powder products in the makeup order.

  • Best for: Dry · Normal · Photography · Professional looks
  • How to apply: Swipe directly below the cheekbone hollow. Blend immediately with a damp beauty blender using stippling (not wiping) motions — work fast before the formula sets. Apply under the chin for jawline definition. Set with a translucent powder after blending to prevent sliding. For combo skin, always set cream contour with powder to prevent melting on the T-zone.
  • Common mistake: Blending too slowly — cream contour grabs once it starts to dry. Work in small sections.
🎨
Bronzer vs. Contour

Often confused but fundamentally different in purpose and tone. Knowing the difference prevents the common mistake of using the wrong product for sculpting.

  • Bronzer: Warm-toned, often shimmer-infused. Used to add sun-kissed warmth and colour to the face — not to sculpt or shadow. Applied to where the sun would naturally hit: forehead, cheeks, nose. Can have a golden or peachy tone.
  • Contour: Cool-toned, always matte. Used to create the illusion of shadow and depth — hollowing cheeks, sharpening the jaw, slimming the nose. Never warm or shimmery.
  • Rule: Bronzer adds warmth and life to the face. Contour adds structure and definition. You can use both — bronzer first for warmth, contour second for sculpt, highlight last for dimension.
  • For your undertone: With neutral-to-cool undertones, lean toward cool-toned contour (taupe, grey-brown) to avoid looking muddy. Warm bronzers like Marigold Peach (NPure) work for warmth on the cheeks.
👤
Contouring by Face Shape

The placement of contour and highlight varies depending on your face shape — the goal is always to create balance and the illusion of an oval shape.

  • Oval: Already balanced — minimal contour needed. Light contour below cheekbones and at temples. Highlight centre of forehead, nose bridge, and chin.
  • Round: Contour the sides of the forehead, below cheekbones, and along the jawline. Highlight the centre of the forehead and under-eye area to elongate.
  • Square: Soften the corners — contour temples, jaw corners, and sides of forehead. Highlight the centre of the face to draw the eye inward.
  • Heart: Contour the wide forehead and temples. Highlight the chin to balance the narrower lower face.
  • Long/Oblong: Contour the forehead hairline and chin to visually shorten. Highlight horizontally across the cheeks to add width.
📋
Complete Face Sculpting Order of Application
  • Step 1 — Skincare & SPF: Fully absorbed before any makeup.
  • Step 2 — Colour Corrector (if needed): Target dark circles or redness before foundation.
  • Step 3 — Foundation / Cushion: Even base layer all over. Your ESQA Skin Tint, TIRTIR, SKINTIFIC, or TIMEPHORIA.
  • Step 4 — Concealer: After foundation — under-eyes, blemishes, brow bone. ONLYOU applied and blended.
  • Step 5 — Cream Contour (if using): Blend before any powder products.
  • Step 6 — Setting Powder: Second Date N00 Translucent on T-zone and under-eyes to set everything.
  • Step 7 — Powder Contour / Bronzer: Sculpt cheeks, temples, jaw. Always after powder — this is where powder contour shines.
  • Step 8 — Blush: Apply after contour — on the apples of the cheeks and blending toward temples.
  • Step 9 — Highlight: Final face step. Powder highlight on cheekbones, inner corners. NPure Glow Oil pressed on cheeks for liquid glow.
  • Step 10 — Eyes, Brows, Lips: Complete the rest of your look.
  • Step 11 — Setting Spray: Pramy → PIXY → L'Oreal Infallible to lock everything in place.

Mascara & False Lashes

A complete reference for choosing, applying, and layering lash products — from everyday mascara to full false lash sets.

🖌️
Volumising Mascara
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ✓Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ~

Thickens and builds fullness on each lash using a dense, creamy formula. The brush has tightly packed bristles that coat every lash from root to tip. Creates a bold, wide-eyed effect. Best for naturally sparse or thin lashes.

  • Best for: Thin · Sparse · Short lashes · Dramatic everyday look
  • Wand shape: Thick, full, cylindrical brush · Dense bristles
  • How to apply: Wiggle the wand at the base of the lashes and pull upward in a zigzag motion — this deposits the most product at the root where volume is needed most. Apply 2–3 coats, letting each coat dry for 30 seconds before the next. Wipe excess off the wand before the first stroke to prevent clumping.
  • Combo skin tip: Look for oil-free, waterproof-friendly formulas — skin oils can travel up to the eye area and cause mascara to smudge under the lash line throughout the day.
↕️
Lengthening Mascara
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

Extends lashes with a fibrous or silicone-enriched formula that grips each lash and draws it out. Creates a fanned-out, separated, elongated effect. The brush is usually a thin comb or tapered wand for precise separation.

  • Best for: Short · Already full lashes · Natural, clean-girl look
  • Wand shape: Thin comb · Tapered · Hourglass
  • How to apply: Start from the root and draw the wand upward in a slow, steady stroke — don't rush. Use the tip of the wand for lower lashes and inner corner lashes. Comb through with a clean spoolie between coats to prevent clumping and maintain separation.
  • Layering tip: Apply lengthening mascara first, then a coat of volumising mascara on top for the best of both formulas — length and body simultaneously.
🌊
Curling Mascara
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ✓Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ~

Contains polymers that hold a curl in the lashes throughout the day, lifting the eye and making it appear more open and awake. Especially useful for straight or downward-pointing lashes that lose curl quickly.

  • Best for: Straight · Monolid · Hooded lashes
  • How to apply: Always curl lashes with a lash curler before applying mascara — mascara makes lashes brittle and curling after can cause breakage. Hold the curler at the base for 10 seconds, then at the middle for 5 seconds, then at the tips for 5 seconds (triple-curl technique) for a natural, gradual curve rather than a 90° kink. Apply mascara immediately while the curl is warm and fresh.
  • Pro tip: Warm your lash curler for 5–10 seconds with a hair dryer before use — the warmth sets the curl more effectively, like a hair curler on a cooler temperature.
💧
Waterproof Mascara
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ~Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ✗

Uses wax-based or silicone polymers that form a water-resistant film around each lash. Holds curl, prevents smudging in humidity, sweat, or tears, and resists the natural oils that cause regular mascara to migrate below the eye.

  • Best for: Humid climates · Oily skin · Long events · Jakarta daily wear
  • Important: Always use a dedicated oil-based eye makeup remover for waterproof mascara — never tug or rub. Soak a cotton pad, press over closed lashes for 10–15 seconds, then gently slide downward. Repeated tugging without proper removal causes lash breakage over time.
  • Combo skin tip: Waterproof formula is highly recommended for combination skin in Jakarta's humidity — oils from the T-zone can transfer to the eye area and cause even regular mascara to smudge by midday.
🌸
Tubing Mascara
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

A different technology — instead of coating lashes with pigment, tubing mascara forms tiny polymer tubes around each individual lash. The "tubes" slide off cleanly with warm water and gentle pressure, leaving zero panda eyes throughout the day. No smudging, no flaking.

  • Best for: Sensitive eyes · Contact lens wearers · Oily skin · Anyone prone to smudging
  • How to remove: Wet fingertips with warm water, press gently over closed lashes and slide downward. The tubes come off whole — you'll see tiny cylinders on your fingertips. No makeup remover needed.
  • Combo skin tip: Excellent choice for oily-lid skin types — the tube formula doesn't interact with skin oils the way traditional wax mascaras do, making it one of the most smudge-proof options for combination skin.
✨
Mascara Application — Master Technique

Regardless of formula, the technique you use determines 80% of the final result. These fundamentals apply to every mascara type.

  • Pull, don't pump: Never pump the wand in and out of the tube — this pushes air in, dries out the formula faster, and creates clumps. Instead, swirl the wand inside the tube to pick up product.
  • Wiggle at root: Start the wand at the very base of the lashes and wiggle back and forth before pulling upward — this deposits product where it matters most and prevents the "spider leg" look at the tips.
  • Lower lashes: Hold the wand vertically and use the tip of the brush in a light dabbing motion. Apply after upper lashes to avoid transfer.
  • Prevent clumping: Comb through immediately with a clean spoolie while the mascara is still wet. Once it dries, don't touch — dried mascara breaks lashes.
  • Apply mascara last: Always do all eye makeup — eyeshadow, eyeliner, and brows — before mascara. Fallout from eyeshadow can stick to wet mascara and create a mess.
  • With PIXY liner: Apply PIXY Eyeliner Pencil before mascara. The wiggle motion of mascara application can transfer liner if it hasn't set yet — give liner 30 seconds to dry before applying mascara.

✦ False Lashes

Types of False Lashes

🌾
Strip Lashes
SkillIntermediateOccasionsDatesEventsNight Out

A full strip of lashes on a single band applied across the entire lash line in one piece. The most common and beginner-accessible type. Reusable up to 15–25 times with proper care.

  • Styles: Natural · Wispy · Dramatic · Mega Volume · Cat-eye · Doll-eye
  • How to apply:
    1. Measure the strip against your eye and trim from the outer edge if too long — never the inner corner.
    2. Apply a thin layer of lash glue to the band. Wait 30–45 seconds until the glue becomes tacky (not wet) — this is the most important step. Wet glue slides; tacky glue grips.
    3. Look downward into a mirror. Place the lash strip as close to your natural lash line as possible, starting from the centre, then pressing down the inner and outer corners.
    4. Press along the band with a lash applicator or the pointed end of a tweezer. Hold for 10 seconds.
    5. Once set, apply PIXY Black liner along the lash band to conceal any glue and seamlessly blend the false lash with your natural lash line.
  • Common mistake: Applying glue when still wet — always wait for it to turn tacky first.
🌿
Individual / Cluster Lashes
SkillBeginnerOccasionsEverydayDatesEvents

Small clusters or individual lash tufts applied to specific areas of the lash line rather than all at once. More natural looking and customisable than strip lashes — you control placement and density.

  • Styles: Individual single lashes · Micro-clusters (3–5 lashes) · Lash clusters (6–10 lashes)
  • How to apply: Apply a small dot of lash glue to the base of each cluster. Let it become tacky. Using tweezers, place clusters just above your natural lash line (not on the skin — on the lashes themselves for the most natural look). Start from the outer corner working inward. Concentrate clusters at the outer third for a cat-eye lift, or place throughout for even fullness.
  • Best for: Natural, customised looks · Filling sparse outer corners · Building toward strip lashes gradually
  • Removal: Soak in oil-based remover or micellar water. Never pull — gently slide the cluster off with a saturated cotton bud at the base.
🏷️
Lash Styles by Eye Shape

Choosing the right lash style for your eye shape creates harmony rather than working against your natural features.

  • Almond eyes: Most versatile — nearly every style works. Cat-eye and wispy styles are particularly flattering.
  • Round eyes: Cat-eye strips with longer outer corners elongate and add elegance. Avoid round doll-eye styles that can make eyes look wider.
  • Monolid eyes: Criss-cross wispy styles create dimension and the illusion of a crease. Avoid overly thick or heavy strips that can weigh down the lid.
  • Hooded eyes: Shorter-length, natural styles — heavy volume can disappear under the hood. Concentrate length at the outer corner to open up the eye.
  • Deep-set eyes: Natural, lightweight styles — avoid very dramatic or heavy lashes that can make eyes appear to recede further.
  • Wide-set eyes: Longer inner-corner lashes balance by drawing attention inward toward the nose bridge.
🧼
False Lash Care & Removal

Proper care extends the life of reusable lashes and protects your natural lashes during removal.

  • Removal: Soak a cotton pad in oil-based makeup remover. Press gently along the lash band for 20–30 seconds to dissolve the glue. Peel from the outer corner inward — never rip from the inner corner, which is closest to sensitive eye tissue.
  • Cleaning: Peel the dried glue off the band gently with tweezers. Dip a cotton bud in micellar water and swipe along the lash band to remove mascara or makeup residue. Never soak the full lash in water — it warps the shape.
  • Storage: Return to the original tray after cleaning — this maintains the lash curve. Store in a dry, dust-free case.
  • Natural lash care: Always apply a lash serum or conditioning oil (like the NPure Glow Up Oil) to your natural lashes after removal — extended false lash wear can weaken the natural lash roots over time. Give your lashes rest days between applications.

Arched Brows
Face ShapeRound ✓Square ✓Oval ✓Long ~Heart ~

The most classically elegant brow shape — features a defined peak at the arch point above the outer iris. Creates immediate lift and a polished, put-together appearance. The arch height determines the intensity of the look.

  • High arch: Dramatic, editorial, striking — adds drama to minimal makeup

  • Best for: Round · Square · Long face shapes (lifts and elongates)
  • How to achieve: Using Azarine Eyettention, draw hair-like strokes following the natural brow direction up to the arch point. Tail should angle slightly downward from the arch peak — never lower than the starting point of the brow head. Feather and blend the front with a spoolie.
  • Common mistake: Placing the arch too close to the inner corner — this creates an angry expression. The arch should sit above the outer edge of the iris or slightly beyond.

Straight / Flat Brows
Face ShapeLong ✓Oval ✓Oblong ✓Round ~Square ~

Minimal to no arch — the brow runs horizontally across the face with a subtle or completely flat curve. A defining feature of Korean and East Asian beauty aesthetics. Creates a youthful, soft, approachable expression and can visually shorten a long face.

  • Best for: Long · Oval · Oblong face shapes · Youthful aesthetic

Face ShapeAll Face Shapes ✓

The "your brows but better" approach — enhances and fills existing brow growth without imposing a structured shape. Looks effortless and uncontrived. Hair strokes mimic real brow hairs so precisely that no one can tell you're wearing product.

Brow Mapping — The 3-Point Method

The universal technique for finding where your brows should start, peak, and end — creating perfectly symmetrical, face-flattering brows every time regardless of shape preference.

  • Point 1 — Start: Hold a pencil vertically alongside the outer edge of your nose. Where it intersects the brow is where your brow should begin. Hairs before this point can be removed or left for a fuller look.
  • Point 2 — Arch: Angle the pencil from the outer edge of your nostril through the outer edge of your iris and extend upward. Where it meets the brow is your arch peak. Mark lightly.
  • Point 3 — End: Angle the pencil from the outer edge of your nostril through the outer corner of your eye and extend upward. Where it meets the brow is where your tail should end.
  • Application: Connect the three points with light feathery strokes — the shape between these anchors is your personalised ideal brow for your face structure. Fill using your Azarine, Luxcrime, and Hanasui pencils in layers.
  • Symmetry check: After drawing both brows, hold a mirror at arm's length and compare — it's easier to spot asymmetry from a distance than up close.
✂️
Brow Maintenance — Grooming Guide
  • Tweezing: Always tweeze after a warm shower when pores are open — less painful, cleaner removal. Pull in the direction of hair growth. Remove one hair at a time and step back frequently to check symmetry. Only remove hairs clearly outside your mapped brow shape.
  • Trimming: Comb brow hairs upward with spoolie. Any hairs that extend above your desired brow shape should be trimmed with small, curved scissors — cut parallel to the upper brow edge, never straight across. Over-trimming is the most common grooming mistake.
  • Threading vs. waxing: Threading offers more precision — ideal for detailed arch shaping. Waxing removes larger areas faster but can remove too much if done quickly. Both should be done by a professional for the initial shaping — maintenance tweezing at home after.
  • Regrowth: Put down the tweezers for 6–8 weeks to let all hairs grow back fully before re-shaping. Over-plucked brows need this reset period. Fill with Hanasui Light Brown during regrowth to keep brows looking intentional.

✦ Lip Shape Encyclopedia

Types of Lips & Techniques

Understanding your natural lip shape helps you choose the right technique — whether you're enhancing what you have or creating an entirely new look.

💋
Full / Pillowy Lips

Naturally plump with balanced volume in both upper and lower lips. The most versatile canvas — virtually every lip technique and product type works beautifully. Both lips are well-defined and proportional.

  • Best techniques: Any product works — from a sheer tint to a full bold lip
  • Enhance naturally: Apply SOMETHINC Ceraplump (Valentine) all over for plumping + SPF. Top with NPure Glow Oil for a juicy, amplified fullness.
  • Bold lip: Apply Time Phoria Cherrion in thin layers from the outer edges inward. Full lips carry bold colours effortlessly — no liner needed for definition.
  • Avoid: Overlining dramatically — full lips don't need it and over-lining can look unnatural on an already full shape.
🌙
Thin / Narrow Lips

Less natural volume with a thinner profile. Upper and lower lips are close in size but with less projection and fullness. Can appear to recede with dark or matte colours. Respond extremely well to plumping techniques and strategic colour placement.

  • Techniques to add volume:
  • Overlining: Using a lip liner just outside the natural lip line — no more than 1–2mm. Follow the natural shape; don't create corners where there aren't any.
  • Ombre technique: Apply a darker shade at the outer edges and a lighter or nude shade at the centre — creates the illusion of projection and depth.
  • Your products: SOMETHINC Ceraplump is ideal — the ceramide plumping formula visibly increases lip fullness over time. Apply NPure Glow Oil at the centre of lips for a dimensional, forward-projecting highlight. Avoid very dark, flat matte colours which minimise apparent lip size.
  • Avoid: Lining inside the natural lip line · Very dark matte shades with no centre highlight · Overlining the corners (creates a clown effect)
🔼
Top-Heavy Lips (Fuller Upper)

The upper lip is significantly more prominent than the lower lip. Uncommon naturally — usually the lower lip is fuller. When the upper dominates, the lips can appear pouty but unbalanced.

  • Balancing technique: Apply a slightly lighter or brighter shade on the lower lip to visually bring it forward. Add a touch of NPure Glow Oil or Glad2Glow Pumpkin Frost only on the lower lip centre to create the illusion of more volume below.
  • Liner: Follow the natural upper lip edge precisely without overlining. You can very subtly overline only the lower lip to bring it forward and balance both halves.
  • Shade choice: Medium-depth cool tones like Time Phoria Cherrion or SOMETHINC Valentine balance naturally — neither too light (which emphasises the upper lip more) nor too dark.
🔽
Bottom-Heavy Lips (Fuller Lower)

The most common natural proportion — lower lip is naturally more voluminous than the upper. A beautiful, sensual shape that responds well to techniques that enhance the upper lip without diminishing the lower.

  • Enhancing technique: Focus slightly more product on the upper lip. Overline only the upper cupid's bow — enhancing the Cupid's bow peaks and the centre of the upper lip creates balance with the fuller lower lip.
  • Cupid's bow definition: Use Azarine or a fine lip liner to draw two sharp peaks at the Cupid's bow before filling in. This creates a structured upper lip that balances the natural fullness below.
  • Highlight trick: A small tap of Glad2Glow Pumpkin Frost at the centre of the upper lip catches light and visually draws the upper lip forward to meet the fuller lower lip.
  • Best shades: All of your lip products work — this is a naturally balanced and beautiful shape. Time Phoria Cherrion's stain formula is especially flattering, concentrating colour at the bow for a natural gradient.
🏹
Defined Cupid's Bow

A sharply defined double-peak at the centre of the upper lip. A classically beautiful and highly desirable lip shape associated with elegance and precision. Enhancing the Cupid's bow is one of the most impactful lip techniques.

  • To enhance an existing bow: Use a fine-tipped liner or the Azarine Eyeppuccino pencil repurposed as a lip liner in a matching shade — define each peak with a precise V shape. Connect the peaks across the centre of the upper lip in a smooth curve.
  • To create a more defined bow: Apply concealer to blur the natural upper lip edge first. Draw a sharper, more defined bow shape on top. Fill with your lip colour — Time Phoria Cherrion applied with a small brush gives the most controlled bow definition of all your products.
  • Highlight: A tiny touch of Glad2Glow Pearl Sparkle (clear shimmer) on just the two bow peaks catches light and dramatically sharpens the Cupid's bow shape — an advanced technique with a stunning result.
↔️
Wide / Narrow Lips (Width)

Width of the lips in relation to the face affects which techniques create proportion. Wide lips extend toward or beyond the outer corners of the eyes. Narrow lips sit well within this boundary.

  • Wide lips: Avoid lining to the very outer corners — keep the colour slightly inside the natural corner for a more proportional look. Deeper, rich shades like Time Phoria Cherrion or SOMETHINC Sangria work beautifully — they add intimacy and bring focus to the centre. Avoid very bright or light shades which emphasise width.
  • Narrow lips: Slightly overline the outer corners of both lips — just 1mm outward — to create a wider appearance. Apply lipstick or stain all the way to the natural corner for maximum width. Bright, warm shades like Glad2Glow Pumpkin Frost draw attention outward and create a wider impression.
  • Gradient technique for both: Concentrating the deepest colour at the centre of the lips and blending outward to a lighter shade creates a forward-projecting illusion that de-emphasises width and adds dimensional fullness.

Lip Techniques

🎨
Gradient / Ombré Lips
SkillBeginnerOccasionsEverydayDatesEvents

A Korean-inspired technique where colour is concentrated at the centre of the lips and diffuses outward, creating a soft, dimensional, blurred effect. One of the most wearable and universally flattering lip looks.

  • How to achieve:
    1. Apply SOMETHINC Ceraplump (Valentine) all over lips as a base — blurs the lip line and adds moisture.
    2. Apply Time Phoria Cherrion concentrated only on the centre of the lips using your fingertip, dabbing in circular motions. Do not pull toward the edges.
    3. Press your lips together gently and re-dab the centre to intensify the colour.
    4. Top with a small amount of NPure Glow Oil or Glad2Glow Pumpkin Frost at the very centre only for a lit-from-within gradient finish.
  • Effect: Makes lips appear naturally flushed and fuller — the dark centre creates the illusion of depth and projection.

SkillBeginnerOccasionsAll Occasions

Apply lip colour, press lips together, then blot with a tissue. Repeat once or twice. The result is a lived-in, effortless flush of colour that looks like naturally tinted lips with exceptional all-day wear.

  • How to achieve: Apply Time Phoria Stellar Dust (Cherrion) in one thin layer. Press a tissue between your lips and blot firmly. Reapply a second thin layer. Blot again. The stain formula grips and stays — the blotting removes the top layer of excess product and leaves the stain behind.
  • Longevity: The blotted stain technique is the most transfer-resistant lip look possible — eating, drinking, and hours of wear barely affect it. Top with Nivea Smoky Rose for comfort without losing the stain.
  • Finish: Matte-stained, natural, effortless. Add Glad2Glow Pumpkin Frost on top to convert from matte stain to glossy stain.

✦ Powder Encyclopedia

Types of Makeup Powder

Powder is the most misunderstood makeup category — there are over a dozen distinct types, each with a different purpose, formula, and technique. Knowing which to use (and when) is the key to a polished, long-lasting finish.

🌫️
Translucent / Setting Powder
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ~Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

A colourless, ultra-fine powder that sets foundation and concealer without adding coverage or shifting the colour of your base. The most essential powder for all skin types. "Translucent" means it works on any skin tone.

  • Purpose: Sets liquid/cream products · Prevents creasing · Extends wear · Reduces transfer
  • Best for: All skin types as a setting step · Combination skin on T-zone specifically
  • How to apply: Using a large fluffy brush, swirl and tap off excess, then lightly dust over foundation. Apply with a velour puff pressed (not rubbed) under the eyes for baking. Use a light hand — translucent powder can still look powdery or flatten skin if over-applied.
  • Baking technique: Press a generous amount under eyes with a damp puff, leave 3–5 minutes while completing other makeup, then dust off. Sets concealer for all-day crease-free wear.
  • Your product: Second Date N00 Translucent Loose Powder — the finest option in your collection. N00P Rose Radiance adds a pink-brightening effect for fairer skin.
  • Combo skin tip: Apply only on the T-zone and under eyes. Leaving the cheeks unpowdered maintains a natural, skin-like finish on drier areas.
🪨
Pressed Powder
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ✗Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ~

Finely milled powder compressed into a compact. Provides light coverage, controls shine, and evens skin tone. More portable than loose powder and easier to apply on-the-go. Can be used as a standalone base on low-coverage days or over foundation for setting.

  • Purpose: Light coverage · On-the-go touch-ups · Mattifying · Portable setting
  • Best for: Oily · Combination · Quick application days
  • How to apply: Press (don't rub) the included puff into the pan and pat onto skin in a pressing motion. For sheerer application, use a fluffy powder brush. As a touch-up, always blot excess oil first with Clean & Clear Oil Control Film before reapplying — pressing powder over oil creates a cakey buildup.
  • Coverage note: Pressed powder is buildable but reaches a ceiling — more layers create cake rather than coverage. For full coverage, always start with liquid or cushion foundation underneath.
  • Shade matching: Choose 1 shade that matches your skin tone (not translucent) for coverage. Translucent pressed powder is purely a setter with no coverage benefit.
🌟
Loose Powder
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ~Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

The same formula as pressed powder but in a loose, free-flowing state. More finely milled than pressed, gives the most airbrushed, professional finish of any powder type. Preferred by makeup artists for its blendability and natural look.

  • Purpose: Professional setting · Extreme airbrushed finish · Baking · Long-wear events
  • Best for: All skin types · Photography · Events · Combination skin
  • How to apply: Dip a large fluffy brush or velour puff, tap off all excess inside the container before removing. Apply with light sweeping or pressing motions. For baking — pack heavily under eyes with a damp sponge. Never blow excess powder off your face as it redistributes unevenly.
  • Storage note: Always tap the lid before opening to settle the powder and prevent spillage. Store upright. Sieve caps dispense evenly — don't remove the sieve.
  • Your product: Second Date Loose Powder (N00 Translucent) — the star of your collection. Used correctly it creates a flawless, non-cakey finish over any base.
🌅
Bronzer Powder
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

A warm-toned powder — golden, peachy, or brown — that adds a sun-kissed warmth and healthy colour to the face. Not for sculpting or shadowing (that's contour's job). Creates the appearance of a natural tan and a warm, sun-touched glow.

  • Purpose: Warmth · Healthy glow · Sun-kissed colour · Complement blush
  • Best for: All skin types · Fair skin with warm or neutral undertones
  • Placement (the 3 rule): Apply in a "3" shape on each side of the face — starting at the temple, sweeping down the cheekbones, and curving to the chin. Also apply lightly across the nose bridge and forehead for a natural sun-touched look.
  • Shade selection: For fair skin (your range), choose a bronzer only 1–2 shades deeper than your skin tone. A bronze too dark reads as muddy rather than warm.
  • Undertone rule: For neutral-to-cool undertones, choose a cool-toned or taupe-warm bronzer — avoid orange-heavy formulas that clash with pink undertones. Peach-gold bronzers are the most flattering.
  • Application brush: Large, fluffy, dome-shaped brush — swirl and tap off excess before applying.
🌸
Blush Powder
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

Pigmented powder in pink, peach, berry, or coral tones applied to the cheeks to add colour, warmth, and a healthy flush. One of the most impactful products for creating a youthful, lively appearance — skin without blush can appear flat and dull.

  • Purpose: Adds life and colour · Completes any makeup look · Creates facial dimension
  • Placement by look:
  • Natural flush: Smile and apply on the apples of cheeks, blend upward toward temples. Round, youthful appearance.
  • Lifted/sculpted: Apply from the temples sweeping across the cheekbones without hitting the apples. More angular and editorial.
  • Draping: Apply blush across the cheekbones and lightly up to the temples and eye socket — a bold, fashion-forward monochromatic technique.
  • Shade for your undertone: Cool-neutral undertone (your profile): rose, mauve, cool pink, berry. Warm shades: peach and coral. Avoid very orange-toned blushes which can clash with cool-neutral skin.
  • Brush: Angled or dome blush brush for precise placement · Fluffy brush for blended diffused flush.
🌑
Contour Powder
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ~Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

A matte, cool-toned brown powder 2–3 shades deeper than skin tone used to sculpt and define facial structure by creating the illusion of shadow. Always matte — any shimmer reads as highlight, not shadow. The most technique-dependent powder in makeup.

  • Purpose: Sculpt · Define · Add shadow and depth · Slim features
  • Placement: Below cheekbones (in the hollow) · Temples · Hairline · Jawline · Sides of nose
  • How to apply: Use an angled contour brush or a small fluffy blending brush. Apply in the hollow below the cheekbone (suck in cheeks to find it) and blend upward toward the temples. For the jaw, apply underneath the jawline and blend downward. Blend until no harsh lines remain — contour should be undetectable up close.
  • Shade rule for your skin: Fair-light skin needs a very light hand and a taupe-grey-brown (not orange-brown) contour. Too dark a shade on fair skin reads as dirt, not shadow.
  • Order: Always apply contour after setting powder but before highlighter.
✨
Highlighter Powder
Skin TypeOily ✗Dry ✓Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ~

A shimmer or glitter-infused powder that catches and reflects light at the highest points of the face. Creates a luminous, three-dimensional, lit-from-within glow effect that makes skin appear elevated and radiant.

  • Purpose: Adds light · Creates dimension · Lifts features · Luminous glow
  • Placement: Tops of cheekbones (highest point) · Inner eye corners · Brow bone · Bridge of nose · Cupid's bow · Tip of nose (optional)
  • How to apply: Fan brush for precise placement or a small tapered brush packed with product then pressed onto cheekbones. The less you blend, the more intense the highlight. For a diffused glow, blend with a fluffy brush. For a blinding highlight, pack with a flat brush or fingertip.
  • Shimmer size guide: Fine shimmer = natural everyday glow. Medium shimmer = glam events. Chunky glitter = editorial/creative looks only.
  • Undertone pairing: Gold/champagne for warm-neutral (your Vanilla base days) · Silver/pearl for cool days (17C Porcelain). Rose gold works for both.
  • Combo skin tip: Apply highlighter only on dry cheekbone areas — never on the T-zone or areas prone to oiliness, where shimmer amplifies rather than illuminates.
🌸✨
HD / Micro-Fine Finishing Powder
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ~Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

An ultra-ultra-fine powder with particles so small they're virtually invisible to the naked eye and camera alike. Specifically engineered to blur fine lines, pores, and texture without adding coverage or weight. Originally developed for high-definition film and television.

  • Purpose: Blurs pores and texture · Invisible finish · Flash-photography ready · No-powder look
  • Best for: All skin types · Photography · Video · Naturally glowy looks where you don't want visible powder
  • How to apply: Always use a fluffy brush with the very lightest possible hand. Less is genuinely more — HD powders are designed to be barely there. Apply only where needed (T-zone and under eyes). Over-application creates a chalky, white-cast effect especially in flash photography.
  • Flash warning: Some HD powders contain silica that causes a white cast in flash photography. Always test before an event with a camera flash.
  • Difference from translucent: HD powder blurs texture; translucent powder sets and mattifies. HD is finishing; translucent is setting. Use translucent first, HD last if desired.
🍌
Banana Powder
Skin TypeOily ~Dry ~Combo ~Normal ~Sensitive ~

A yellow-toned loose powder originally formulated to colour-correct purple under-eye circles on deeper skin tones. On fair skin, it adds a warm, brightening effect and cancels any redness beneath the eye. The name comes from the yellow-banana hue.

  • Purpose: Colour-corrects purple/blue under-eye circles · Brightens under eyes · Sets concealer with a warm-brightening effect
  • Best for: Medium to deep skin tones (primarily) · Fair skin for light brightening and purple circle correction
  • How to apply: Pack under the eye over concealer using a flat puff or brush. Let sit 3–5 minutes (baking), then dust off. On fair skin, use extremely sparingly — too much creates a visible yellow cast. Use only if you have strong blue-purple under-eye circles to correct.
  • For your skin: On your fair-light neutral-to-cool tone, use only under eyes and with a very light touch. A micro-dusting adds subtle warmth and corrects circles without making skin look yellow.
💜
Colour-Correcting Powder
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ✓Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

Tinted powders that use colour theory to neutralise and correct specific skin concerns before or after foundation. Each colour targets a different issue on the face — more buildable and forgiving than liquid correctors.

  • Green: Cancels redness, blemishes, and broken capillaries
  • Peach/Salmon: Brightens under-eye circles (fair to medium skin)
  • Yellow: Neutralises purple veins and blue circles (banana powder)
  • Lavender/Purple: Brightens sallow or yellow-toned skin — adds a cool, luminous finish (especially useful over Vanilla-toned bases)
  • White: Brightens, lightens, and extends other powders. Use sparingly — flash photography risk.
  • How to apply: Apply after foundation but before setting. Use a small, precise brush. Apply lightly over the specific area needing correction — apply the matching skin-tone powder over the top to tone down the corrector colour and blend seamlessly.
🔮
Finishing / Luminising Powder
Skin TypeOily ✗Dry ✓Combo ~Normal ✓Sensitive ✓

The very final powder step — applied over completed makeup to add a veil of radiance, blur any remaining imperfections, and unify all the products on the face into one cohesive, polished look. Often contains light-diffusing particles.

  • Purpose: Unifies makeup · Adds a final luminous veil · Blurs texture · Refines the overall finish
  • Difference from setting powder: Setting powder locks products in place and mattifies. Finishing powder is applied last over everything for an aesthetic effect — glow, blur, or unification.
  • How to apply: Large fluffy brush, very light dusting all over the face — the lightest hand of any powder in your routine. This should add a dimension or glow, not be visible as product. Apply after highlighter and blush.
  • Combo skin tip: Luminising finishing powder is best applied only on dry cheek areas. Keep the T-zone powder-free at this step or it will look overly powdered by the end of your routine.
💎
Baking Powder (Technique)
Skin TypeOily ✓Dry ✗Combo ✓Normal ✓Sensitive ~

Not a specific product type — baking is a setting technique using any loose or translucent powder applied in a generous amount and left on the face for several minutes before being dusted off. Originally a drag and stage makeup technique now widely used.

  • How it works: Body heat "bakes" or sets the concealer and foundation beneath the powder layer while the excess powder sits on top. When you dust it off after 3–5 minutes, the makeup underneath is perfectly locked and any creasing is prevented.
  • Best areas for baking: Under eyes (over brightening concealer) · T-zone (over foundation to prevent shine) · Chin
  • How to apply: Dampen a beauty blender or velour puff. Press into Second Date N00 Translucent Powder. Press firmly onto under-eye area — do not blend, just press and leave in place. Complete your eye makeup and brows. After 3–5 minutes, use a large fluffy brush to dust off the excess in downward strokes.
  • Combo skin tip: Baking under the eyes and on the T-zone gives combination skin the longest-lasting, crease-free base possible. This is an essential technique for long events or humid days in Jakarta.

Makeup Removal Tutorial

The most important step of your day — done properly, in the right order, without damaging your skin.

✦ Why Proper Removal Matters for Combination Skin

Leaving makeup on overnight — even just a little — clogs pores, traps sebum in your T-zone, and causes breakouts. For combination skin, improper removal is one of the biggest triggers for both congestion and dryness. The double cleanse method removes every layer without stripping the skin barrier. Never tug, rub, or use harsh wipes as your only method.

1
Remove Eye Makeup First
Micellar Water or Dedicated Eye Makeup Remover
Always start with the eyes. Saturate a soft cotton pad with micellar water or an oil-based eye makeup remover. Hold the pad gently over the closed eye for 10–15 seconds — don't rub immediately. Let the product dissolve the eyeshadow, mascara, and brow product first. Then use a soft downward swipe to remove. For stubborn eyeshadow (especially the JADED darker shades 08 and 09), use a pointed cotton bud soaked in remover to target the outer V and lower lash line precisely.
Never rub the eye area. The skin there is the thinnest on your face and repeated tugging causes premature fine lines and sagging.
2
Remove Lip Color
Oil Cleanser or Micellar Water on Cotton Pad
The Time Phoria Stellar Dust Lip Stain (Cherrion) and the NPure Glow Up Oil are both long-wearing — don't try to wipe them off dry. Apply a small amount of oil cleanser or balm directly to the lips, massage gently in circular motions with your fingertip for 20 seconds to dissolve the stain and any lip balm layers, then wipe away with a soft damp cotton pad. Repeat if the Cherrion stain has deeply set. Your lips should feel soft and clean, not sore.
Lip stains are designed to resist wiping — work with oil, not against the formula. Patience here saves your lip skin from dryness and micro-abrasions.
3
First Cleanse — Oil/Balm Cleanse
Oil Cleanser or Cleansing Balm (Dry Hands, Dry Face)
With dry hands on a dry face, scoop or pump a generous amount of oil cleanser or cleansing balm and massage all over the face in slow, circular motions for a full 60 seconds. This breaks down the cushion foundations (TIRTIR, SKINTIFIC, TIMEPHORIA), concealer (ONLYOU), setting sprays (PIXY + L'Oreal), and the NPure face oil. Pay extra attention to: hairline, around the nose, jaw, and any areas where powder has settled. The T-zone needs longer massage — makeup mixes with sebum and sits deeply in pores there. Emulsify with a few drops of water until it turns milky, then rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water.
Don't skip this step even on "light makeup" days. Sunscreen alone requires oil cleansing to remove properly — water-based cleansers alone cannot break down SPF or silicone-based formulas.
4
Second Cleanse — Water-Based Cleanser
Gentle Foaming or Gel Cleanser
After the oil cleanse, follow with a gentle water-based cleanser to remove any remaining oil residue, leftover product, and deeper surface debris. Lather in palms first, then apply to face with gentle circular motions for another 30–45 seconds. Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water — never hot (it strips the skin barrier and worsens oil production in the T-zone). Pat dry with a clean, soft towel — never rub. For combination skin, this second cleanse targets any leftover foundation pigment in pores that the oil cleanse may have loosened but not fully rinsed.
After double cleansing, skin should feel clean and comfortable — not squeaky, tight, or stripped. Tightness = barrier damage = more oil production.

Micellar Water + Cotton Bud for Touch-Ups
Under good lighting, inspect: hairline (powder and foundation often accumulate here), sides of the nose (hardest area to fully cleanse — cushion and skin tint settle into pores here), the under-chin area, and ear perimeter. Use a cotton bud with micellar water for precision cleanup in these spots. A common mistake is thorough face cleansing but missing the hairline — leaving residue here causes hairline breakouts over time.
Check your towel after patting dry — if it shows significant color transfer, your cleanse wasn't complete. Re-cleanse the areas that transferred.

Hydrating Balancing Toner
Immediately after cleansing, apply a hydrating toner while skin is still slightly damp to restore skin's pH balance. Cleansing temporarily raises skin's pH — toner brings it back to the ideal slightly acidic level (4.5–5.5) where the skin barrier functions best and where your subsequent skincare products absorb most effectively. For combination skin, pat toner into skin with palms rather than cotton pads — this uses less product and is gentler. Apply an extra press on drier cheek areas.

Continue with PM Skincare
Essence → Treatment Serum → Eye Cream → Zone-Targeted Moisturizer
With a clean, pH-balanced canvas, proceed with your evening skincare: hydrating essence, treatment serums (niacinamide on T-zone, retinol or peptides all over), eye cream tapped around the orbital bone, and zone-based moisturizer (lightweight gel on T-zone, richer cream on cheeks). For combination skin, this zone-targeted approach is essential — don't apply the same product thickness everywhere. Skin repairs itself most actively between 10pm–2am, so earlier cleansing means better treatment absorption.

✅ Removal Do's

  • Always double cleanse after any SPF or makeup
  • Remove eye makeup before face cleansing
  • Use oil first on dry face — never wet
  • Be patient — let products dissolve before wiping
  • Check hairline, jaw, and nose sides carefully
  • Apply skincare within 60 seconds of cleansing
  • Cleanse every single night without exception

⚠️ Removal Don'ts

  • Don't use makeup wipes as your only removal step

  • Don't skip removal because you're tired

Mid-Day Touch-Up Guide

Keep your makeup fresh and your skin comfortable through Jakarta's heat and humidity.

✦ Combination Skin Mid-Day Rule

The golden rule: always BLOT before you touch up. Adding more powder over oil creates a cakey, uneven mess. Blot first, then lightly touch up only where needed. Your PIXY mist can refresh the whole face without disturbing makeup. Less is more for midday — you're refreshing, not reapplying.

1
🧻 Blot the T-Zone First

Use Clean & Clear Oil Control Film — gently press (never rub) onto forehead, nose, and chin. The blue micro-powder film absorbs excess oil and leaves a soft finish. This is always the FIRST step of any midday touch-up. Never skip this or you'll layer powder over oil and create cakey texture.

2
💧 Refresh with Mist

Mist Pramy Moisturizing Setting Spray or PIXY Aqua-Beauty Protecting Mist over your face from 20–25cm distance. Close eyes and spray in T then X pattern. This refreshes the look, controls any remaining shine, and revives makeup without disturbing it. Let air dry naturally. Do this even if you don't do any other touch-up steps.

3
🌸 Light Cushion Touch-Up

If coverage has faded significantly, use the cushion puff (clean side) to very lightly pat TIRTIR or SKINTIFIC only on areas that need it — usually under-eyes and around blemishes. Don't press hard or you'll disturb the existing makeup. Pat gently 2–3 times at maximum. Avoid over-foundation of the T-zone.

4
✨ Powder Only if Needed

If T-zone is still shiny after blotting and misting, apply a tiny amount of Second Date Translucent Powder only on the oiliest spots with a small brush. Use the lightest hand possible — 1–2 gentle sweeps. Avoid the cheeks entirely unless they have dry patches needing setting. Over-powdering mid-day is the most common mistake for combination skin.

5
💋 Refresh Your Lips

Reapply SOMETHINC Ceraplump Tinted Lip Balm (Valentine) for SPF protection and fresh color. If wearing Time Phoria Cherrion stain, reapply a thin layer and blot with a tissue. Top with Glad2Glow Pumpkin Frost for a fresh, luminous finish. The Nivea Smoky Rose is great for a quick, low-maintenance refresh that also moisturizes.

6
✦ Final Seal

Finish midday touch-up with one more light spritz of L'Oreal Infallible 3-Second Setting Mist to seal everything back together and give a freshly-applied finish. Hold at arm's length, mist lightly, and allow to dry. This is the magic step that makes midday touch-ups look seamless rather than layered.
