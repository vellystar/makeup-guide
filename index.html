Understanding Your Canvas

Combination Skin

Combination skin features an oily T-zone (forehead, nose, chin) alongside normal-to-dry cheeks. The key to flawless makeup is choosing products that balance oil control without over-drying — exactly what this collection is designed to do.

✦
T-Zone Behaviour

The forehead, nose, and chin produce excess sebum, leading to shine, enlarged pores, and potential breakouts. These areas need oil-controlling and mattifying products.

◇
Cheek Zones

Cheeks may feel tight, flaky, or normal. They need lighter hydration and gentle coverage that doesn't cling to dry patches or emphasise texture.

❋
Key Concerns

Uneven texture, pore visibility, midday shine, foundation separation in the T-zone, and maintaining adequate SPF protection throughout the day in Indonesia's climate.

◈
Product Strategy

Lightweight, buildable formulas with pore-blurring and setting technology. Cushion foundations and serum tints deliver controlled, skin-like coverage without overloading pores.

Know Your Tone

Understanding Undertones

Your skin tone is what you see on the surface — fair, medium, deep. Your undertone is the subtle hue beneath the surface that never changes, no matter how tan or pale you get. Choosing foundation, cushion, or BB cream shades that match your undertone is the difference between makeup that disappears into your skin and makeup that looks "off."

How to Identify Your Undertone

There are several reliable methods. Use more than one test — the more results that align, the more confident you can be in your undertone.

  • Vein test: Look at the veins on your inner wrist in natural light. Greenish veins indicate warm undertones; blue or purple veins indicate cool undertones; a mix of both indicates neutral. Olive-green veins with a muted tone suggest olive undertones.
  • Jewellery test: Which metal makes your skin look more alive and luminous? Gold flatters warm and olive; silver flatters cool; both look good on neutral undertones.
  • White paper test: Hold a plain white sheet of paper next to your bare face. If your skin appears yellowish or peachy next to white, you're warm. If it looks pinkish, bluish, or rosy, you're cool. If it looks greyish-green with little pink or yellow, you may be olive. If you can't tell, you're likely neutral.
  • Sun test: Does your skin tan easily to a golden-brown (warm/olive), or does it burn and turn pink before tanning (cool)? Neutral skin does both moderately.
  • Colours that flatter: Earthy tones, oranges, warm reds, and yellows look stunning on warm/olive undertones. Blues, purples, emerald greens, and cool pinks suit cool undertones. Neutrals can wear almost anything.
Why Undertone Matters in Makeup

A foundation that matches your depth (lightness/darkness) but not your undertone will appear ashy, orange, too pink, or "mask-like." This is why two people with the same surface skin tone can wear completely different shades of the same product.

  • Cushion foundations: Shade letters like "C" (cool), "W" (warm), or "N" (neutral) indicate the undertone direction. Always match the letter to your undertone first, depth number second.
  • BB creams and skin tints: Warm-named shades like "Vanilla," "Honey," or "Sand" typically carry a warm-neutral finish. "Ivory" or "Porcelain" shades are usually cool or neutral.
  • Setting products: Translucent powders and clear mists are universally undertone-free. Tinted powders, however, can shift tone — always check if they're warm, cool, or neutral-tinted.
  • Sunscreens: Physical/mineral sunscreens can leave a white cast on deeper or cool skin tones. Tinted physical SPFs exist to counteract this. Chemical sunscreens are generally cast-free.
  • Layering effect: When layering products (BB cream + cushion + powder), undertone can stack and intensify. Mixing warm + warm layers can look overly orange; mixing warm + cool can neutralise to a clean finish.
Warm
Yellow · Peachy · Golden

Warm undertones have a golden, peachy, or yellow cast beneath the skin surface. This is extremely common in Southeast Asian, South Asian, Middle Eastern, and Latin skin tones. The skin naturally appears sun-kissed and glowing even without makeup.

How to Identify

Gold jewellery makes skin look radiant

Look For Shades labelled "Vanilla," "Honey," "Beige," "Sand," "Golden," or with "W" undertone codes. Avoid shades with "C" codes or names like "Porcelain" (cool) — they may appear ashy, chalky, or overly pink on warm-toned skin.
Cool
Pink · Rose · Bluish

Cool undertones carry a pinkish, rosy, or bluish hue beneath the skin. Though less common in Southeast Asia, cool-toned Indonesian skin does exist — particularly in those with fairer complexions, or where natural flushing and rosiness is visible.

How to Identify
Veins appear blue or purple
Silver jewellery enhances the complexion
Skin appears pink, rosy, or ruddy naturally
Skin burns more easily before tanning
Blues, purples, cool pinks, and emeralds flatter most
In Makeup
Look For Shades coded "C" (cool), or named "Porcelain," "Ivory," "Rose Beige," or "Blush." Avoid heavily warm shades like "Golden Vanilla" or "Honey" — they'll pull orange or too yellow on cool skin, making the complexion appear muddy.
Neutral
Balanced · Beige · Balanced Mix

Neutral undertones are an even balance of warm and cool — no single hue dominates. This is often described as the most "flexible" undertone category because a wide range of shades work well. Many people believe they're neutral when they may actually be olive — the two are often confused.

How to Identify
Veins appear blue-green — a mix of both
Both gold and silver jewellery complement equally
Skin doesn't skew noticeably pink, yellow, or green
Most clothing colours seem to work reasonably well
Skin tans moderately without burning or going golden
In Makeup
Look For Shades coded "N" (neutral) or labelled "Natural," "Nude," "Beige," or balanced "Vanilla." Neutral skin has the most flexibility — you can often wear both warm and cool shades depending on the look. When in doubt, "N" codes are always the safe starting point.
Olive
Green · Muted · Earthy

Olive undertone is a distinct category often misclassified as neutral. It has a muted, greyish-green cast beneath the surface — common in Mediterranean, Southeast Asian, Middle Eastern, and mixed-heritage skin. Olive tones can appear either warm or cool depending on the light, making shade-matching particularly tricky.

How to Identify
Veins appear distinctly green, sometimes grey-green
Skin has a greyish, earthy, or muted quality in certain light
Gold and earth-tone jewellery is most flattering
Cool-toned shades make skin look sallow or ashy
Very warm shades can pull orange — balanced warmth is key
In Makeup
Look For Neutral-warm shades or shades specifically designed for "olive" tones. Avoid both extremes — very cool shades make olive skin look ashy and grey, while overly warm/orange shades clash with the greenish cast. A slightly warm neutral (like "Vanilla" or "Natural Beige") is often the best starting point.

Lips & Undertone — What to Know

Lip colour interacts with your natural lip pigment, so the final shade is always a blend of the product and your own lip tone. Warm undertones typically have deeper natural lip pigment, which can deepen or shift a shade on application. Cool undertones often have lighter, pinker natural lips that allow sheer formulas to show truer to the swatch. Neutral and olive lips fall in between — formulas perform close to the tube colour.

  • Warm lips: Corals, peaches, nude-browns, and warm reds are most flattering. Cool pinks and blue-reds can read ashy or brash.
  • Cool lips: Berry, mauve, rose, cool nude, and blue-based reds harmonise beautifully. Warm corals and oranges can clash.
  • Neutral lips: Nearly any shade works — the natural lip tone acts as a blending canvas. Experiment freely.
  • Olive lips: Warm nudes, terracotta, brick, and muted mauves are most flattering. Very cool pinks can pull strange against the earthy undertone.
  • Lip prep matters: Exfoliate lips 2–3× per week and always apply a nourishing treatment underneath colour. Well-prepped lips make every formula last longer and look smoother.
How to Layer Your Lip Products

These four products layer together harmoniously. Here is the recommended order, from most treatment-forward to most colour-intense:

  • Step 1 — Glad2Glow Brightening Lip Serum: Always first. Apply as your treatment base and allow 30 seconds to partially absorb. The serum plumps and smooths for better colour payoff from everything above it.
  • Step 2 — NPure Glow Up Oil (Marigold Peach): Worn alone over the serum for a glossy plumped look — or applied as the final topper over any colour product for a dimensional, glossy-stain finish.
  • Step 3 — Nivea Rich Care & Color (Smoky Rose): Worn alone for a comfortable wash of tinted colour, or applied as a conditioning base layer before the stain to prevent dryness and cracking beneath it.
  • Step 4 — Time Phoria Stellar Dust Stain (Cherrion): The highest-pigment step — your statement colour layer. Apply as the final colour step, then press (don't swipe) the NPure oil on top once it has set for maximum wear and gloss.

Expert Advice

Combination Skin Tips

01
Zone-Specific Application
Apply mattifying and oil-controlling products (powder, matte mist) only on the T-zone. On dry cheeks, use lighter, more hydrating layers to prevent tight, flaky coverage.
02
Layer Thin, Not Thick
Multiple thin layers of foundation always outperform one heavy layer. Build gradually — it prevents caking, creasing, and formula separation that combination skin is prone to.
03
Blot Before Anything
Always blot excess oil with tissue or oil-blotting paper before any midday touch-up. Applying powder or mist over oil significantly reduces longevity and creates uneven texture.
04
Cushion Hygiene Matters
Use the clean reverse side of the cushion sponge for midday touch-ups — a used sponge carries bacteria and oxidised product that can disrupt your base and cause breakouts.
05
Mist Order Is Everything
Apply setting mist after powder (to bind and meld layers), then SPF spray as the absolute final step. This order ensures maximum hold while keeping UV protection on top where it works.
06
Hydration is Non-Negotiable
Well-moisturised skin under makeup lasts longer everywhere — including oily zones. Dehydrated skin overproduces oil to compensate, making your T-zone worse, not better.
07
Match Cushion to Occasion
SKINTIFIC for glow and events; TIRTIR for longevity on busy days; ESQA Skin Tint for effortless natural days. Matching the formula to your schedule keeps skin looking intentional and fresh.
08
Clean Tools = Better Skin
Wash your cushion sponge weekly, powder brush every 2 weeks, and wipe the spray bottle nozzle with alcohol monthly. Clean tools extend product performance and protect combination skin from breakouts.
